2 Introduction A mighty artifact is hidden in a forgotten temple. An obscure spell makes it unreachable. The only way to break this arcane magic is to find the tombs of the Evil Champi - ons, which rest in the temple, evoke them, and defeat them all! Find the artifact and escape from the temple before all evil creatures dwelling there wake up! Explore an ancient dungeon, avoid traps, kill the monsters, challenge bosses, grab treasures, gain experience points, and  improve your character's abilities. Temple of Horrors is a dungeon crawler game for 1-4 players. "The Forgotten Artifact" introduced above is just one of the  available adventures! Each adventure will play differently every time, providing hundreds of game sessions for infinite  enjoyment! "This game is my tribute to my wonderful Dungeons and Dragons game sessions in the '80s" (M. Quondam)." Overview Players must venture into a dungeon where various  creatures dwell. Among them, Minion Creatures (such  as Goblins, Spiders, Skeletons, and Specters) are the least  dangerous, but their numbers pose a challenge. Elites  and Leaders are more formidable and appear when too  many Minions have been alerted. Finally, Bosses repre - sent a significant risk as they guard specific locations  and possess unique and powerful abilities. To create the Dungeon map, players place Room tiles from  the Room display. The placement of these tiles is crucial.  For instance, players can activate Clue Rooms and Tomb  Rooms by connecting multiple Rooms of the same color.  Both types of Rooms provide valuable clues that can lead  to the discovery of important hidden elements, such as an  Artifact, a Prison, a Treasure, and more. Players will face additional challenges in the dungeon.  Guard Posts are present, housing packs of creatures that  remain dormant until the slightest noise awakens them,  triggering an attack. Moreover, unexpected events like  the collapse of the temple roof can block the heroes'  path. To open the Exit in such cases, players may receive  a Map card, revealing an "Inspiration path." By creating  this specific path on the dungeon map, the Exit will acti - vate and open, allowing the players to proceed. Choose your hero from a selection of seven characters:  Barbarian, Cleric, Thief, Bard, Mage, Monk, and Ranger.  Each hero possesses a unique deck of cards, with each  card representing a distinct power. At the beginning  of the game, players start with an active card and can  acquire new cards by spending their Experience Points. Additionally, each hero can equip various items. These  item cards can be discovered in treasures or purchased  using Mystic coins. It's time to assemble your party of heroes, set up the  game, and choose your adventure! 8 Game Phases The game plays in Hours and ends at the end of the eigh - teenth Hour or when the Game End conditions are met. Each  Hour is separated into 5 phases and played in the following  order: 1.   Hour Start : Move the Hour token on the Hour track and  apply the Period conditions.  2.   Initiative:  Roll the initiative.  3.   Actions:  This Phase lasts several Action Rounds until the  Actions phase End conditions are met. In each Action  Round Heroes and Creatures can do 1 Action and any  number of Free actions. 4.   Encounters:  Do an Encounters check and place new  Creatures on the Dungeon map. 5.   Hour End phase : Do the Game recovery procedures.   1. Hour Start phase Follow these steps: 1. (Excluding the first Hour) Move the Hour token. The Lead  Player moves the Hour token on the Hour track by 1 space  from left to right. If the new space contains the symbol  ,   discard Item cards from the Item card display and place 5 new  cards drawn from the Item deck. Apply the Game Period Conditions.  The Game Track spac - es are in 4 colors, corresponding to 4 Game Periods: green,  yellow, orange, and red. The Green Period expires, and the  Yellow Period begins: 2. Initiative phase The Lead player rolls all the  Initiative cubes  on the table at  once. There is 1 Initiative cube for each player (Hero color), 1  for Creatures (black), and 1 for Melee (red).  Considering the Dungeon Map coordinates, the Lead player  sorts the rolled cubes by their position from the North to the  South and places them in a line. Each cube in the line rep - resents an Initiative segment: Each Hero acts at their Initiative  segment, starting from the left to the right. Creatures act at  their Initiative segment (black cube). The Melee Actions are  done at the Initiative segment represented by the red cube. The Lead player rolls the Initiative cubes and places them in a  line following the North-South order. The resulting Hour order  is (from left to right): the Blue player, then the Melee Actions  take place, then the Yellow player plays, then the Creatures, and  finally the Orange and Green players. Game Modalities Cooperative Mode In this modality, all players join their efforts to reach the  Adventure aim. If a single Hero completes the Adventure, all  players win. Do not use the PvP deck for this modality. Semi-Cooperative Mode In this modality, all players join their efforts to reach the Ad - venture aim, but only those who complete it win. If 2+ Heroes  end the Adventure, there is a tie victory. In other cases, a single  Hero wins. Do not use the PvP deck for this modality. PvP Mode A player could be the Traitor and act against other players.  The Traitor role is secret, so players do not know if there is a  Traitor and who it is. It’s also possible that no Traitor will be in  the game, as the PvP deck contains as many Hero cards as the  amount of players +1 traitor card. •   Back to life  rule is not used. •   Players cannot reveal their PvP card until the game end. If  their hero dies, they still have to keep it hidden. •   The Traitor wins alone. Traitor-winning conditions are  explained in each Adventure description.  •   No-Traitor players play in a Semi-Cooperative mode. •   A Hero can attack and kill another Hero. At the end of the  game, it should be considered that a loyal Hero can only  kill the Traitor: if he kills another loyal Hero, he has lost the  game. The Traitor Hero can kill anyone instead. In PvP mode, players interact with each other almost as if in  a role-playing game. There are no game elements that allow a  player to uncover a traitor, except through their behavior and  way of speaking. This mode is designed for those who enjoy  interaction and a touch of theatricality. It increases the game  difficulty, so consider it when choosing the adventure.  Hero vs. Hero Combat When a Hero attacks another Hero with Weapons (Melee or  Ranged), each Hero rolls. Apply damage points done and re - duce the damage points suffered by the dodge points gained.  Do the same in a Ranged attack, but apply damage only to the  Hero receiving the attack in this case. Example I: A Thief attacks in Melee a Cleric; both Heroes roll and  the results are: 7   and 0    (Thief) and 4   and 3   (Cleric). The  Cleric takes 3   (7-4=3) and inflicts 3   on the Thief (3-0=3).   Example II: A Ranger performs a Ranged attack against a Bar - barian; both Heroes roll: 6   and 2   (Ranger) and 2   and 3    (Barbarian). The Barbarian takes 3   (6-3=3). Campaign mode You can use the same Hero for more than a single Adventure!  You can do it with any Adventures if they are not written to be  played in sequence.  •   Back to life rule  is not used.  •   If your Hero survives, count your XP (the XP value of your  cards and Stats) and coins value (your coins+the value in  coins of your items): Divide these numbers by 2 (rounded  down): these are your scores for the Next Play.  “Survive”  means that your Hero exited the Dungeon.   Then reset your Hero: discard or downgrade your Power  cards and Stats to fit the Next Play XP number. Discard  your items or coins to match the Next Play coins number  (you can sell your Items in this process). Then discard any  Magic Shards and start the next Adventure at full HPs.  •   If your Hero dies and a Hero of another player survives,  and you want to continue the Campaign, you can re-use  your Hero in the next adventure with half XP of a Hero  that survived and 1 coin. •   If you succeed in an Adventure, you have to  increase the  next Adventure difficulty by 1  (see the " Introduction " in  the  Adventures Booklet ). Variants Multiclass Hero Skilled players can play with a character combining two Heroes’  characteristics. It’s possible to start a game with a multiclass  Hero or add the second Hero type later. Use these special rules: •   Use both Heroes decks, but only 1 starting Power card  (choose).  •   Use the average HPs value (round down).  For example, a  Mage-Cleric (3 HPs and 6 HPs = 4.5 HPs) starts with 4 HPs.  •   Keep any to hit or dodge penalty of both Hero classes. •   Choose a Combat style and defensive Slots of one of the  two Heroes and use their Hero card over the other. If the  other class has it, you can acquire the other Combat style,  spending 4 XPs. •   Every time you activate a new Hero power, you must spend  1 additional XP point. Note: This extra XP is paid for the  card space on the table , not for the card level.  For example,  if you activated two new Hero cards, you had to pay 2 addi - tional XPs, while to activate one new Hero card and then to  upgrade it to level two, 1 additional XP only.  If the card cost  is zero (red star), you still have to pay 1 XP. •   If you started with a regular Hero and want to add the  characteristics of a second Hero, you must have the addi - tional XPs available to pay the new Hero card spaces.
