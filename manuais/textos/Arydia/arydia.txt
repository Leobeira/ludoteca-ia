7 Skill Tree The skill tree contains all the skills you can learn in your chosen path.  You may look at the skills and rules cards to understand what the skill  does before you choose to learn it. The black arrows on the skill tree  represent the prerequisites for a skill. If a skill has one or more arrows  pointing to it,  all prerequisite skills must be learned first  (but they  don’t need to be equipped, nor mastered)  before learning the new skill. Learning Skills When taking the Level Up action, you learn  any one skill for which you meet all the  prerequisites. Pop the skill out of the skill tree,  and place it Learned side up, into a slot in your  character mat. If there are no open slots, you  may immediately unequip a skill to make room  for the new skill. Skills represent special abilities your character possesses. You can have  up to 6 active skills total: your 5 active path skills plus your 1 character  skill. You begin the game with 3 skill tiles: 2 for your chosen path and 1  for your chosen character. You  cannot exceed the MAX RP, as set by the current party size. • d20: Keep your d20 here when you’re not rolling it. 1 2 3 4 5 6 7 Items There are many items that players can earn in the game. These items are  generally used to increase the power of individual exiles. For example,  a new sword might make the warrior more deadly in combat, while a  new piece of armor will protect you from foe attacks. Some items are  not directly offensive or defensive and include things like Kna psacks  and Potions. Items are divided into  Pathless  and  Path  items. Pathless Items: These are identified by the brown ‘A’ icon.  Pathless items are generic and can be used by  any path. These can be purchased from town,  earned as rewards for defeating foes, or found  in Pathless chests. Path Items: These are identified by the colored Path icons.  Path items are specific to each exile’s path and  can only be used by exiles of that path. These  items can only be gained from Path Chests  found in the world. Pathless Chests When you gain a Pathless chest,  1 item total is drawn  randomly from  the corresponding Pathless chest. One  exile gains the item (Pathless items  may be traded freely among party members). Path Chests When you gain a Path  Chest, each exile gains  1 item  at random from  their corresponding  Path chest. Empty Chest If you would draw from a chest,  but the chest is empty, gain 3  Kae  instead of gaining an item. Drawing Items When you draw an item from a  chest, you must choose blindly from  the items. However, you may feel around for an item tile of a specific  size. 10 Checks (d20 System) As with many traditional pen-and-paper RPGs, Arydia uses a d20  (twenty-sided die) system to simulate things that happen in the game  world. Checks are used throughout Arydia to determine outcomes of a  wide variety of actions, events, and experiences. The basis of a check  is rolling your d20 and adding your character’s matching attribute.  Depending on the result, different outcomes are possible. There are four  types of checks: Success / Fail, Threshold, Tiered and Non-Attribute.  Success / Fail Checks 1 STR  DEX 12 INT 1 WIS 2 CHA  END Success / Fail checks have you roll the d20 and add your exile’s  specified attribute to the roll to get your result. If your res ult  equals or  exceeds the check , you are successful - go to the green  . If your  result is less than the check, go to the red  . For brevity, sometimes checks may look like this:  12 STR , 2 WIS , and success / fail may look like this:  and  . (Example, INT 5 check, a result of 1-4 = Fail, a 5 or higher = Success) Threshold Checks These checks have you roll the d20  and add your exile’s attribute to get  your result. Unlike Success/Fail  checks, these checks have multiple, cumulative thresholds. As your  rolls reach higher thresholds, each lower threshold is also included in  the result. These checks are most common in weapons with attack  patterns (STR Threshold check for this attack pattern: A  1 would be a failure, a 2-9 would lead you to the white  outcome, a 10-19 to the white + light gray outcome, and  20+ leading to white + light gray + dark gray outcome.) The current mode of the game is displayed on the face-up  side of the Mode token: Adventure mode comprises all of the  interactions you have that are not inside  of Combat; if you’re not in Combat  mode, you’re in Adventure mode. Turns Adventure mode is intended to be more free-form than Combat. Players  should generally take turns doing actions and encourage everyone  to be involved. However, they should also be flexible and allow for  situations in which it makes more thematic sense for one player to take  consecutive actions. When in doubt, err on the side of what is fun and  thematic. Adventure Mode Actions Players may take the following actions while in Adventure mode,  depending on what is available at their location. We will describe these  in more detail in the following sections. • Travel:  While on a Worldmap, move from hex to hex. • Enter/Leave Locations:  While on a Worldmap, enter a location, or while in a location, leave and return to the Worldmap. • Interact:  You can interact with pointers wherever you encounter them on any component (card, map, or Worldmap.) • Skills: You can use any applicable skills (some may only be used in Combat). Some skills specify ‘Out of Combat’ and can only be used while in Adventure mode (read specific skill rules cards for more details). • Items: You can use any applicable items (some may only be used in Combat). • Trade:  You can give Pathless items or Kae to another exile. • Equip: You can equip / store items from your inventory. • Move:  While in a location, you may move any number of squares, while still respecting impassable borders (unlike in Combat, where your movement is more limited). • Explore:  While in a cleared location, you can draw a new map that’s connected to your current map. • Context Specific:  Some actions are only available in special circumstances, and rules text will describe these actions. We’ll now go into more detail on Adventure mode, after which we’ll  look at Combat mode. Unlike the more free-form turn structure in Adventure mode, Combat  mode carefully tracks character turns, movement, and actions. When  one or more foes are revealed, flip the Mode token to its Combat  side:  the game is now in Combat mode.  Start of Combat When combat begins, first take these steps: 1. Shuffle all cards in the Threat Deck. 2. Reset  Threat and  Combo to 0. 3. Resolve any ‘Start of Combat’ rules or abilities. Combat now takes place over a number of  rounds , with foes and exiles  taking turns  moving on the  map  and fighting each other until  combat ends . Rounds Combat proceeds in rounds, and each round has two parts: 1. Foe Turn  (draw one Threat card, possibly activating foes) 2. Exile Turn  (one exile takes actions) For the first round, the player who triggered combat goes first. They  take a turn for the foes, followed by taking their exile turn.  (Example: a  player takes the ‘Explore’ action revealing a map with foes - they have  triggered combat and after the Foe Turn, they will act first.) If combat has not ended,  play passes to the player on the left in  clockwise order , and another round begins. (That player takes a turn  for the foes followed by their exile turn.) End of Combat:  Combat can end in one of several ways: • The exiles defeat all the foes. • All exiles are knocked out  at the end of an Exile Turn. • The exiles flee the combat. • Or other ways specified by that combat. (In many cases, you’ll  wish to interact with a pointer more than once, such as returning to an  NPC after discovering something. Leaving these cards out while you  explore the location can help the game flow more naturally.) Combat Terms These are common terms used in combat on various abilities: • Adjacent: You’re adjacent to anything in your square, in a square  that shares a traversable border with your square, or if it’s in a  corner of your square.  • Exile:  Any exile, including the exile using the ability. • Ally:  Any other exile, not including the exile using the ability. • Foe:  An individual foe, represented by a single miniature. • Creature:  Any miniature, including exiles and foes. • Shift: Forcefully shift the target a number of squares in any  direction (this is not a move, so ignore   costs). The creature may  not enter occupied squares, and cannot cross Impassable or Wall  borders. If a foe is using the ability, shift the creature as far as  possible. If an exile is using the ability, they may choose how far to  shift the creature (up to the maximum allowed by the ability). ◦ Shifting Large Creatures:  Large creatures do not change  orientation when shifted. Large creatures may enter multiple  squares simultaneously; as above, the creature cannot cross  Impassable or Wall borders. • Push: Same as shift, but each square entered must be further from  the origin of the effect. • Pull: Same as shift, but each square entered must be closer to the  origin of the effect. • Unoccupied Square:  There are no creatures in the square. • Empty Square:  No creatures, tokens or markers in the square. 28 Hit Dice There are four Hit Dice:   Red,   Blue,   Yellow,   Gray. The face of each die contains squares with pips in the center; these  show you the possible hit locations for that die. The black square  on each face is the location on your body where the hit lands. White  squares with no pips denote locations that cannot be hit by that die. When a foe makes an attack against you: 1. Roll the associated hit die to determine where on the body the hit  lands. ◦ Multiple targets:  The hit die is only rolled one time, regardless  of the number of targets hit by the attack. All targets take the full  damage value of the attack. 2. Locate the hit location on the body: if you have armor in that  location with durability  remaining, add one Wear token to that  armor and reduce the incoming damage by its defense   value. 3. Any remaining damage is subtracted from your  HP. If you reach  0  HP, you are  KO’d . Foe Attacks  A foe attack is a named ability on the foe’s AI card that can do   damage via a hit die. After moving into position, if the foe is in range of the target, it will  now resolve its attack. If there is no valid target within range, the  foe will skip this part of the behavior, and go to the next part of the  behavior, if there is any. Foe attacks usually look like this: • Name:  This is the keyword used in the foe behavior to call this  attack. • Hit Die:  The specified hit die rolled for this attack. • Attack Value:  This is how much damage this attack will do to each  target. • Positional Grid:  Most attacks have a grid, but not all.  (see: Positional Grids p.21) • Ability Type:  Melee, Ranged, or Area. 1 2 3 4 5 Foe Abilities  Some foes have abilities that are not attacks. These abilities can be  active abilities or ongoing abilities. ● Active (black text)  are usually part of a  behavior. ● Ongoing (red text)  always apply to that  foe. a b Exile Armor Your armor provides protection from foe attacks while it has durability   remaining. Each piece of armor can have Wear tokens up to its  durability  value. When hit, if a piece of armor has durability  remaining, add  exactly one Wear token  to that armor to reduce the  incoming damage by its defense  . A piece of armor absorbs damage  only once per attack. Once a piece of armor has Wear tokens equal to  its durability  , it is broken and you cannot add any more Wear tokens  to it. It will no longer reduce damage until you repair the Wear tokens,  which you can do at towns.  • Defense Resolutions:  Some skills and items allow you to absorb  damage. When multiple abilities of items, skills, etc., trigger at the  same time, players choose how to resolve them. If all damage is  absorbed by other abilities before a piece of armor would be hit, do  not place a Wear token on that armor. • Fully Blocked:  Some abilities check to see if the damage was  “fully blocked” or not. Damage is fully blocked if you lose  0  HP.  If you lose  1  or more HP, then it was not fully blocked. 32 There are a variety of attack patterns. Attack patterns are applied to a  Hit Grid one damage at a time. You begin by choosing where to apply  the first point of damage, with all subsequent damage keyi ng off of  the previous damage. When applying damage, the orientation of the  Hit Grid and the direction of the attack pattern are important.  Attack patterns cannot be rotated. (e.g. An attack pattern that goes from left  to right will always be applied from left to right on the Hit Grid) . First Point of Damage The first point of damage determines where the attack pattern st arts on  the Hit Grid, and will either be Directional or Aimed.  First Point: Directional This is identified by the black arrow. Depending on the direction of the  arrow, choose a row (if the arrow is horizontal), a column (if vertical),  or a diagonal (if diagonal) that contains at least one unwounded Hit  Point. Trace a line, starting at the edge of the card along the path to the  first unwounded Hit Point  in the chosen row, column, or diagonal, and  apply the damage. That is the path the first point of damage travels -  any Criticals passed through are triggered.  First Point: Aimed This is identified by the black X. If the first point of damage i n the  attack pattern is aimed, it can be applied to any unwounded Hit Point  on the Hit Grid. Aimed damage cannot trigger Criticals (because it does  not travel along any path). Following Damage After the first point of damage is applied, any additional dama ge for  that pattern is considered following damage. Following damage is  directional damage that is applied to the Hit Grid by tracing a straight  line from the previous point of damage in the direction indicated by the  following damage. The following damage moves along the line until it  hits the first unwounded Hit Point in that line, applying damage  there,  or it travels off the card, and does not damage a Hit Point. Any Criticals  the following damage travels through are triggered. Bonus Damage Some attacks will trigger bonus damage, either by skills (such as  Mark or Clashing Chorus) or Criticals. Bonus damage is applied  after  resolving the damage from the attack pattern. Wounded Points Any points on the Hit Grid containing a wound are ignored when applying attack patterns. Directional damage passes right through  wounded points, continuing on until it hits an unwounded point. Aimed  damage cannot target wounded points. (e.g. if the other party members have mastered 3 and 4 skills, the  new player may master up to 3 immediately.) • XP: The new character immediately gains XP to match the rest of  the party. • Path Items: The new character draws Path items equal to the other  party members  (same number of items at the same tiers, including  any items that were drawn and subsequently sold) . Drop Out:  To remove a character from the campaign, simply don’t  use that character after any rest. If you subsequently want to return  the character to the campaign, follow the “Drop In” rules, but  starting  from the character’s last saved state instead of level 0.  (e.g. they only  draw items equal to the difference from their current point to the party’s  situation, any skills they previously mastered remain mastered, etc.) Note: We understand it is possible to exploit these rules to gain Kae,  but that goes against the spirit of the game.
