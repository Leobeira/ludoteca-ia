1 Components Chants for the Old Ones is a board game based on the works of H.P. Lovecraft. Players form a cult with the sole purpose of summoning an Old One -  those mighty entities of abyssal antiquity who show utter disregard for this insignificant species called humanity. To achieve your goal, you have to  recruit new followers to your cult and leverage your influence and power in the three vital enclaves of Arkham, Innsmouth, and Kingsport. With enough  summoning power, sufficient arcane knowledge, and the occasional sacrifice, you may even be able to invoke the aid of some horrifying creatures to  rid yourself of those pesky investigators - deluded humans who seek to dismantle your organization from within. Each Old One has its own whims to  be attended to before they heed your call. 2 Overview CULTISTS The Cultists’ ultimate goal is to summon an Old One. To do so, they  form a cult which consists primarily of the cultist cards you gain  throughout the game. Cultists may act in different areas of the board, as indicated. In  some cases, they also have additional abilities that may help you  fulfil your mission. CULT Throughout the game, you form and expand  a cult with the sacred mission to summon  an Old One. Your  cult  consists of the cards  you hold in your  draw  deck, your  hand , and  your  discard pile  (including the cards you are  playing at any given moment).  Your cult may be made up of cultists, some  creatures you are capable of summoning, and  those pesky investigators who infiltrate your  cult causing you various kinds of trouble. CREATURES Creatures are kept in a deck at the  centre of the main board. You may  summon them to use in your cult.  Unlike the Old Ones, they are lesser  creatures, and you may manipulate  them if you possess sufficient arcane  knowledge and, depending on the  creature’s preferences, offer them a sacrifice.  You may summon them to devour those pesky  investigators. OLD ONES The Old Ones are superior  entities of unfathomable  antiquity that completely ignore  the insignificant human race  and its foolish world. The cults  are nevertheless determined to  summon them to demonstrate  their absolute devotion - even  if it means bringing an end to  the world as we know it. Old Ones cards are not part  of your hand. They are the  ultimate aim to win the game  - provided that you have  what it takes to perform the  necessary chants to summon  them (by meeting the  requirements shown on your  Old Ones card). CHANTS Chants are ancient rituals performed by the cults to summon an Old  One. These chants have endured the ages as hidden knowledge in  forbidden books. Your cult will strive to be the first to summon an Old  One, and, in doing so, win the game and bring chaos to this dull old  world. To perform the chants, you need to meet the requirements  shown on your Old Ones card. ARCANE KNOWLEDGE Arcane knowledge tokens represent your  arcane knowledge. They are essential for  summoning creatures. Gain tokens by taking  actions in cities, by taking certain actions by  using cultists, and by establishing temples in  the cities. 

3 CITIES The main action takes place in the three  cities of Arkham, Innsmouth, and  Kingsport, each represented by  the three outer areas of the  main board. In these cities, you will  encounter powerful and  influential individuals,  or those, discreetly  infiltrated in society, who  are drawn to the ancient  cults and are susceptible to  being recruited by your cult.  To recruit new cultists, you  need to exert your power of  influence in the right place.  The cities are also perfect  hubs for establishing temples and to enhance the abilities of your  cult. POWER Power tokens represent your ability to influence  certain spheres of society and attract the  attention of specific individuals to recruit and  make them part of your cult. TEMPLES In each city, there are dedicated  spaces to establish temples by  placing a permanent temple  token on any one of them.  Temples represent your  ability to continuously gain  power and knowledge, as well  as other resources that will  help you summon an Old One.  To establish a temple, you need  to dispatch one of your cultists by  removing that card from the game. INVESTIGATORS Meddlesome busybodies! Investigators are  upright individuals who seek to uncover and  dismantle your cult from within. Whenever  you Act within a city or perform any action,  you attract their attention, and they will try  to infiltrate your cult. Having investigator cards in hand reduces  your gameplay options, so it is always a  good idea to summon a creature to devour  them. Some cunning cultists are also able to  uncover them and offer them as sacrifice. Stealth, on the other hand, represents the ability of  some cultists to avoid detection by potential investigators. Stealth  comes in several guises: as a character’s special ability, as stealth  tokens, and as a special area on the main board where you place  your pawns when using stealth. SACRIFICES Sacrifices are offerings made to summon certain  creatures or Old Ones. You may sacrifice  investigators, but also cultists - everything for  the cause! Some individuals might no longer  be useful to your cult after all. When making  a sacrifice you gain sacrifice tokens (you  may use either cardboard or wooden tokens  interchangeably). Using creatures is the fastest way  to gain them. POWER OF INVOCATION This is the cultists’ power to summon creatures and Old  Ones. Cultists may gather together in groups and even  use creatures to enhance their power of invocation.

6 Playing the Game Object of the Game The first player to perform the invocation chants of their Old One  wins the game. To perform the “invocation chants”, you must  meet the requirements shown on your Old Ones card. For more  information, see “Ending the Game”. Game Turn Overview Players take turns clockwise, starting with the starting player until an  Old One is invoked. During your turn you perform one of the actions below: To Act, play a card from your hand to place a pawn onto an empty space of the main board.  In this example, we played the card of Lavinia Whateley, which allows us to play in different  zones of Arkham (spaces marked with the same city icon). We decide to place the pawn in the  free space next to the deck of cards, the space action allows us to take the top card of the deck,  paying its cost. Both, the card played, and the card gained, go to the discard pile. In doing so,  we apply the card’s ability to gain 4 power points. The Arkham symbol allows us to  place a pawn in different areas of  that city. We choose this space to acquire a cultist. We pay 4 power  tokens as indicated by the  card. Then we place the card  on our discard pile. Play a card from your hand to place one of your pawns or your  personal board on an empty space on the main board. The space  must match the position icon on the card played.  After placing your pawn, you immediately perform the action  indicated by that space on the board. If the card played has an ability, you may perform it before or after  activating the action on the board. Discard the played card by  placing it on top of your discard pile. Act Return Rest Play a card to place a pawn onto the main  board and perform the corresponding action. Retrieve your pawn(s) from the board, resolve  investigators and refill your hand. If you have not played any pawn, refresh  your hand. Act You cannot place a pawn on a space already occupied by another  pawn, regardless of whether it is yours or that of another player. Nor  is it possible to place it on a space if you cannot perform the space  action. In this case, you cannot use the ability of the card in play  either. 

7 Mandatory Return Retrieve your pawns from the main board and put them on your  player board (this is your only possible action if you start your turn  with both your pawns on the main board). An investigator will  infiltrate your cult for each pawn that returns from the main board  to your player board (unless you resolve the encounter by avoiding  the investigator; see Investigator below). Then, you may discard a card from your hand (investigators may  not be discarded in this manner) onto the discard pile on your player  board. Now, draw cards from your deck until you have 5 cards on  your hand, or the number indicated by your hand size (for more  information see Hand size below). Tactical Return  If there is only one pawn left on your player board, you may place  it in the tactical return zone  w  instead of performing Act. Add a  +1-draw token above your draw deck. Then retrieve the pawn from  the main board using Return, as specified above. Finally, return your  pawn from the tactical return zone to its initial position  q . You may only perform this action if you start your turn with both  your pawns on your player board. Place both pawns in the rest zone of your player board  e , then  follow these steps: • Discard all cards from your hand (of any type) to the discard pile  on your player board. • Then, draw cards from your deck to refill your hand. • Return your pawns to their initial positions. • Additionally, you may recover a cultist you have previously  removed from the game (only initial cultists). If you choose to  do so, exchange it for any cultist currently in your cult, and place  the recovered cultist into your hand. Whenever your deck runs out while drawing cards, shuffle your discard  pile, form a new deck with these cards, and continue drawing cards  until your hand is complete.  Your player board: The return zone  q  is at the top right. Pawns occupy these positions during  mandatory return. The tactical return zone  w  is below it, to the left. Placing a pawn here, will  increase your hand size, as indicated. The rest zone  e  is to its right. Sometimes your hand may  be compromised, and you may prefer to Rest on your turn, allowing you to refresh your hand and  maybe even recover an initial cultist that has been eliminated from the game. 11 Hand size Your hand size is 5 cards by default. This means that  when you need to draw cards, you will draw cards  from the deck until you have a total of 5 cards in your  hand. Remember that, before drawing, you may discard a card  from your hand. Some cards and board actions may temporarily modify the size  of your hand. Whenever this happens, place tokens above your  draw deck on your player board. For example, if an action indicates that you increase your hand, place a +1-draw  token above your draw deck. Then, you gain two more hand size increases by  performing another action before it is time to draw. You change your +1-draw  token for a +3-draw token (+1 for the first and +2 for the last action) ... When it  is time to draw cards,  these modifiers apply. If you are supposed to draw and  you have a +3 token above your draw deck, draw cards until you have a hand  of 8 cards (5+3), then, return all draw tokens to the reserve. Although you now  play with 8 cards, the next time you draw cards it will be 5, as usual, unless you  gain more draw modifiers during your next turn (increasing or decreasing your  hand size). It may be possible that you need to draw cards, but your hand is already full.  Continuing with the previous example, if it is time for you to draw and you  already have 6 cards in hand, at a time when your hand size should be 5, you  must not draw any additional cards because your hand is already full (you do  not have to remove any cards from your hand either, even if you have more  than your current hand size). Whenever you deplete your deck while drawing,  shuffle your discard pile, form a new deck with these cards, and continue  drawing cards until you reach your current hand size. Investigators Every time you retrieve a pawn from the main  board to your player board ( Return ), it triggers an  encounter . Resolving an Encounter: Any encounter with an investigator may have one  of two possible outcomes: 1. Investigator infiltration When you retrieve your pawn, you must take the  top card from the investigator deck and place it in  your discard pile. If there are no more cards left in the investigator  deck, you must move the pawn to the stealth zone,  where it remains until your next turn. Some game actions or abilities trigger an  investigator infiltration (such as  Devour in danger  zone ,  Learn ,  Raid , etc.). These do  not  constitute an encounter  (which only occurs when using  Return ) and therefore cannot be  avoided. Concealment : When a pawn returns from the  stealth zone ,  no encounter is triggered, despite using Return for your pawn.  In this zone your movements are so stealthy that no  investigator can detect them. 2. Avoid infiltration Whenever an  encounter  is triggered, you may avoid  the investigator as follows: Stealth token : You may spend  a stealth token to prevent an  investigator from infiltrating your  cult. Stealth card : You may discard a  card with a stealth icon from your  hand to prevent an investigator  from infiltrating your cult. The presence of investigators in your cult  reduces your gameplay options, as they take  up space in your hand but cannot be used to  perform any actions.

13 Ending the game The first player to perform the invocation chants for their Old One  wins the game. 15 Solo-player mode If you are completely new to the game, we recommend you play your first solo-player  game at beginner level. If you have already played a multiplayer game, you can start  at intermediate level. At beginner level, blockade tokens come into play: Whenever an  investigator infiltrates your cult after an encounter, place 1 blockade  token on any area of the main board. This blocks the area and  reduces your scope for actions. At intermediate level, raid comes into play: Whenever you use  Return, add 1 harassment token. For every third harassment token, 1  investigator infiltrates your cult. And, last but not least, at expert level, special investigators come  into play.
