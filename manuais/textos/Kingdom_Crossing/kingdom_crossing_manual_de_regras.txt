Marco Canea & Stefania Niccolini David Sitbon I n a faraway land, in the midst of a verdant forest crossed by the Crystal  River, is the small Kingdom of Brightspring, ruled by the wise Queen  Beavery.  Just in the vicinity of the Kingdom, the river splits, forming four islands,  which are connected to the shores and to each other by seven bridges.  Thanks to these bridges, the inhabitants could reach every region of the  Kingdom. Each region, in fact, offered different resources: in one there  was plenty of food to be found, another was rich in wood, yet another  was conducive to the harvesting of sap... and the seven bridges provided  access to all resources.  For a long time, the Kingdom prospered peacefully; however, dissent  and envy gradually arose among the inhabitants of the various regions.  Therefore, the Queen, concerned about the peace of Brightspring, decided  to make periodic tours of her Kingdom in order to meet with her subjects  and calm their tempers. Out of a sense of fairness and not wanting to  displease anyone, she established a path so that each bridge would be  crossed once and only once: a double passage, in fact, would inevitably  arouse the envy of others, while a missed passage the sadness of some. Thus, following the directions of the Grand Chamberlain in charge of ﬁne- tuning the itinerary, the Queen’s procession departed from the Northern  Region accompanied by the jubilant chirping of the Robins. Her subjects  greeted her with joy but, after crossing the sixth bridge, the Queen realized  that the only way she could complete the tour would be to retrace her  steps and re-cross one of the bridges already traveled. The day, which  began in celebration, ended with long faces and the feeling that they had  made things worse instead of better. In  the  days  that  followed,  the  Grand  Chamberlain  made  numerous  reconnaissance  trips  to  the  Kingdom,  but  each  time  he  noticed  that  something was not working: “I’ve been through here before”, “I forgot the  middle bridge and I can’t go back” were phrases that recurred sooner or  later. The people of the Kingdom, seeing the Grand Chamberlain’s efforts,  felt involved, and every day more and more subjects came to the Court to  propose new solutions, but none of them turned out to be the right one  in the end. And while the Grand Chamberlain and the inhabitants of Brightspring  were studying new paths, the old Mathematician from the Forest came  to tell the Queen that all their efforts were in vain: only an eighth bridge  would make it possible to complete as expected the much-desired tour  without crossing the same bridge more than once. So  the  Queen  instructed  her  most  trusted  counselors  to  gather  the  laborers needed for the construction of the new bridge, not an easy task  since they too had to pass each bridge only once to avoid displeasing  anyone. The paths of the Grand Chamberlain, like those of anyone else, it  was now known, did not serve the purpose. Better to proceed by instinct  and, in case, hope to use the Balloon to reach another Region and, if  possible, get home before the evening shadows fall on the Kingdom. I n Kingdom Crossing, you take on the role of a beaver foreman charged by the Queen to gather craftsmanship to secure the resources and deco - rations needed to build the new bridge. To accomplish this, you must seek the cooperation of the forest animals by going around the Kingdom  without ever being authorized  to cross the same bridge more than once in the same day. The animals of the Forest, divided into ﬁve Guilds, not  only provide you with the necessary resources, but also offer their support in various ways: the guild of the wily Foxes grants you immediate Prestige;  the guild of the wise Owls provides you with money; the guild of the powerful Bears allows you to gain advantages from your structures; the guild of  the impetuous Wild Boars allows you to improve your movement around the Kingdom; and ﬁnally, the guild of the selﬂess Deer offers you additional  lumber, stone, sap, or food. Choosing which turn to take and who to rely on will not be an easy task for you, but it will decide which of you the Queen will entrust with directing  the construction of the eighth bridge. Player board (resources and decorations) Your player board represents your efforts to participate in the building of the 8 th  bridge, so accumulating resources and getting Decorations  will help you on your path to victory! In general, you accumulate resources (  Lumber,   Stone,   Sap and   Food) for  the ﬁnal scoring and for fulﬁlling Bridge goals. However, when you reach a speciﬁc  space on each track, you obtain an immediate bonus. Furthermore, when you reach  the last space of any resource track, you gain a Favor of the Queen . You also place Decoration tiles (Flowers   and Sculptures  ) on  your player board to score your Character cards at the end of the game, only if your  resource marker has passed these Decoration tiles. Bridge goals At the beginning of the game, 6 Bridge goal tiles are placed on the main board: they symbolize the Queen’s demands to build the 8 th   bridge. Each Bridge goal is double-sided, one side being easier  to complete than the other. During your turn, you may validate a  Bridge goal, and the quicker you do it, the more VP you gain. Favors of the Queen Favors of the Queen are personal objectives that score VP at the end of the game. They can be gained in various ways during the  game. Invest Action The Invest track is a way to gain coins: everytime you encounter this icon, choose one of the following:  •  Make your marker progress one space on the Invest track. •  Gain the coins indicated by the position of your marker, then return it to the leftmost space of the track. Coins Any time you gain coins, take them from the common supply. When you spend coins, spend them from your personal reserve and  return them to the common supply. VP Any time you gain VP during the game, advance your Score marker on the Score track accordingly. Endgame VP You score this kind of VP at the end of the game only. How to play The game is divided into 4 rounds, each of them divided into 3 phases: 1) Morning phase:   in turn order, players collect their Income ( A ), then Birds arrive in the forest ( B ), and the Queen awakes (  C  ). 2) Noon phase:  in turn order, each player selects one Action tile and resolves it.  The Noon phase ends when each player has played 4 Action tiles. 3)   Evening phase:  in turn order, if your Worker ends the round in a region with one of your Houses, choose one Income to  collect. Solo rules General concepts for Paco & Paca Paco & Paca do not have a worker on the main board. They simply take cards from 1 or 2 different regions, without spending coins for them. Paco &  Paca do not consider what is depicted on the card: they simply collect Character and Structure cards on their playerboard as you would do. For each Character card they place on their player board, they advance by 1 space the resource marker linked to that guild: Fox guild:  advance the Lumber marker 1 space.    Owl guild:  advance the Stone marker 1 space.    When tucking a Structure card at the bottom of their player board, nothing else happens. If the advanced marker goes beyond an empty space for Flowers or Statues, take the corresponding tile and place it on Paco & Paca’s playerboard,  to ﬁll that space. When advancing their resource marker, Paco & Paca take into account this icon   only and therefore gain a Favor of the Queen when  reaching it. All other icons are ignored by Paco & Paca.  In the solo mode, you play against Paco & Paca, two beaver twins who seek the favor of the Queen like you.
